# Portfolio Rebalancing and Drift

## Target Allocation and Portfolio Drift
A target allocation represents the planned proportions of different asset classes within an investment portfolio. For example, an investor might decide on a specific balance between equities, bonds, and gold based on their risk tolerance and time horizon. Over time, different assets perform differently. Assets that gain value grow to make up a larger percentage of the portfolio, while assets that lag behind become a smaller percentage. This divergence between actual portfolio weights and target weights is known as portfolio drift. If left unattended, drift can inadvertently increase overall risk or reduce alignment with original objectives.

## What Is Rebalancing?
Rebalancing is the process of resetting portfolio proportions back to their designated target allocation. When a portfolio experiences significant drift, rebalancing brings asset weights back into line. Traditionally, this is accomplished through selling and buying: an investor sells a portion of the overweight assets that have grown and uses the proceeds to purchase underweight assets. This disciplined approach enforces a habit of trimming assets when their relative value is elevated and buying assets whose relative value has dipped. However, selling assets may incur transaction fees, trading frictions, or potential tax consequences in taxable accounts.

## Cash Flow Rebalancing Versus Selling
An alternative and efficient method is cash flow rebalancing. Instead of selling existing holdings, the investor uses incoming cash, such as new monthly deposits, to buy exclusively the underweight assets. Over time, directing fresh contributions toward lagging assets gradually pulls the overall portfolio back toward its target allocation without triggering asset sales. Cash flow rebalancing avoids the transaction costs, spreads, and tax liabilities associated with selling. While substantial drift may still require selling and buying when new deposits are insufficient, cash flow rebalancing provides a gentle, low-friction maintenance strategy for regular savers.
