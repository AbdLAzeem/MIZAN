# Understanding Gold

## What Is Gold as an Asset?
Gold is a physical commodity and a precious metal that has served as a medium of exchange and a store of value throughout human history. Unlike stocks or bonds, gold is not tied to the earnings of a corporation or the promises of a government issuer. Investors often look to gold as an alternative asset that behaves differently from traditional paper securities. People can gain exposure to gold by purchasing physical bullion, coins, or shares in pooled funds that track the metal. For instance, the Sahara Gold Fund (SGLD) is a fictional simulation fund designed to hold physical gold bullion on behalf of investors seeking commodity exposure.

## How Gold Gains or Loses Value
The price of gold is determined primarily by global supply and demand dynamics, currency fluctuations, and investor sentiment. Because gold is commonly priced in major fiat currencies, a decline in currency purchasing power can lead investors to bid up the price of gold as a perceived hedge against inflation. During periods of heightened geopolitical tension or broader economic uncertainty, demand for gold often increases as investors seek traditional safe haven assets. Conversely, when economic conditions appear stable, real interest rates rise, or stronger currencies prevail, the demand for gold can diminish, leading to a drop in its market price.

## Lack of Income and Price Volatility
A significant characteristic and risk of holding gold is that it produces no cash flow. Unlike companies that pay dividends or bonds that pay regular interest, a bar of gold generates no ongoing income. An investor only realizes a financial gain if someone else is willing to purchase the metal at a higher price in the future. Additionally, storing and insuring physical metal can involve ongoing costs. Gold prices can also remain volatile or stagnant for extended periods, making it vulnerable to opportunity cost when income generating assets are performing well. Even pooled vehicles like SGLD reflect these price shifts.
