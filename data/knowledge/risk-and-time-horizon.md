# Risk and Time Horizon

## Understanding Investment Risk
Investment risk refers to the degree of uncertainty regarding the future value of a portfolio and the potential for losing capital. Every asset carries some form of risk, whether from daily market volatility, changes in interest rates, unexpected inflation, or business failure. An investor's ability and willingness to endure market swings is often referred to as risk tolerance. While some individuals feel comfortable seeing their account balances fluctuate in exchange for growth potential, others prefer steady valuations that protect their existing savings from short-term declines.

## The Role of Time Horizon
Time horizon is the total length of time an investor expects to hold their assets before needing to withdraw funds for a specific goal. Goals can range from short-term objectives, such as purchasing a home in a few years, to long-term plans spanning decades. Time horizon plays a decisive role in shaping portfolio design. When an investor has a long time horizon, they have more time to ride out temporary market downturns and recover from inevitable cyclical slumps. Conversely, an investor with a short time horizon cannot easily afford sudden drops in value, because they may need cash on short notice.

## Aligning Portfolios with Horizons
Matching portfolio construction to time horizon helps prevent forced selling during market downturns. Portfolios with extended timelines often allocate more weight to volatile growth assets, such as equities, because interim fluctuations are less critical than long-term purchasing power. For shorter horizons, portfolios frequently emphasize defensive holdings, such as cash equivalents and fixed income, where price stability is paramount. A clear understanding of when money will be needed helps investors maintain discipline, avoid panic during market drops, and build an allocation suited to their personal timeline.
